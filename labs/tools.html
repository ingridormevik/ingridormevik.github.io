BEFORE VS CODE. BEFORE CONSTRUCT. BEFORE AI.

The Hall of lab legends

Every tool in this room stands on three people who built computers before electricity ran them. Their machines were made of gears, cards and paper tape. Their questions are still ours.

CBCharles Babbage1791–1871

The engine builder

He designed the Difference Engine (1822) and the Analytical Engine (1837): machines of brass gears that could calculate on their own. The Analytical Engine was meant to be programmed with punched cards.

In this lab: every Construct event is a tiny engine. Condition in, action out.

ALAda Lovelace1815–1852

The first programmer

In 1843 she published notes on Babbage’s engine. Note G is a step-by-step method for calculating Bernoulli numbers, often called the first computer program. She also saw that the machine could work with music and symbols, not just numbers.

In this lab: your JavaScript is a list of steps, just like Note G.

ATAlan Turing1912–1954

The machine thinker

In 1936 he imagined a machine that reads and writes symbols on an endless tape: the idea behind every computer. In 1950 he asked “Can machines think?” and proposed the imitation game.

In this lab: when you code with AI, you are working inside his question.
