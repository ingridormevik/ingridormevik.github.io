LAB 01 / FRIDAY 2 OCTOBER 2026

A place becomes a webpage

How can a detail from your own life shape a digital work?

Morning · 08:15–10:15 · 120 minutes

What you can take from this lab

Leave with: Visible, saved progress on the student’s own project, using today’s technique where relevant; the starter exercise is available for anyone who needs it.

Connect to the course: Digital Creativity: creative practice develops through materials, tools, precedents and other people. Connect a deliberate design choice to the experience you want to create.

Before this session: prepare and check
  • Open the classroom QR and VS Code setup guide. Test Microsoft Live Preview on the teaching computer.
  • Keep a clean copy of the starter page and the CSS example ready. Choose a public webpage to inspect; use the simple starter if its code is too complex.
  • Choose a short, personally comfortable passage from your own work. In The Turing Test, focus on the connection between experience, uncertainty and interaction; do not require students to disclose comparable experiences.
  • Confirm the exact MittUiB task and its submission format before students arrive.
  • Treat the demonstration checklist as a menu: show one small example to the room and use the other items during individual support. Two breaks and own-project time are protected.
Opening words you can use
Hi, I’m Ingrid, your lab assistant and a third-year Digital Culture student. My knowledge is still compiling. I do not know everything, but I am good at finding things out, and I am here to help you do that too. I work across electronic literature, games, research, activism and Mount Media. Often I begin with something I have experienced, then ask what happens when another person can interact with it. You do not need my subject matter or my tools. Bring something you care about, choose a small next step, and we will work from there. You can work without AI, ask basic questions and take breaks. This is a workshop, not a speedrun.
Live demonstration: click-by-click teaching checklist
  1. Open the chosen public page. In Chrome, right-click a heading and choose Inspect. Point to the selected element in Elements and its CSS in Styles. Explain that these are the current page elements; View page source shows the delivered HTML and may differ after JavaScript runs.
  2. Use the simple starter page to read the structure from the top: doctype, html, head, title, body, heading, paragraph, details and summary. Name structure, visual styling and behaviour as different layers; HTML itself can provide a reveal interaction.
  3. In VS Code choose File → Open Folder and open a memorable Desktop folder. Open index.html. Show one text edit, Save, and Show Preview.
  4. Create style.css beside index.html. Add the supplied stylesheet link inside head. Paste the small CSS example, save both files and show the effect.
  5. Change one spacing or colour value and explain its purpose. Ask students to predict the effect before you save.

LAB 02 / FRIDAY 16 OCTOBER 2026

A choice becomes a consequence

What does an audience's action change, and why does that matter?

Morning · 08:15–10:15 · 120 minutes

What you can take from this lab

Leave with: Visible, saved progress on the student’s own project, using today’s technique where relevant; the starter exercise is available for anyone who needs it.

Connect to the course: Creative Coding: rules and state changes can express ideas. AI literacy: distinguish an explanation from evidence that a program actually behaves as claimed.

Before this session: prepare and check
  • Open the JavaScript guide at steps 1–4 and download the starter ZIP. Test the button and a deliberately broken script filename.
  • Keep a clean working example so students can recover without losing their own text.
  • Default to documentation and peer explanation. Only use the optional AI comparison after Alinta has agreed it fits this lab and its task.
  • Do not assume an AI exercise authorises AI in the final assessment or substitutes for another compulsory task.
  • Treat the demonstration checklist as a menu: show one small example to the room and use the other items during individual support. Two breaks and own-project time are protected.
Opening words you can use
Last time you gave an observation a form. Today the audience can do something to it. Before adding code, decide what the action should mean. A button labelled Continue is less interesting than a choice with a consequence you can describe. We will write a small amount of JavaScript, predict what it will do, and test that prediction. You can complete the entire session using the examples, documentation and another person. Understanding your own decision is the important part. You are welcome to work on your own project throughout the working blocks. I am here to assist with whatever helps you move forward. We have two breaks, and you do not need to finish a whole project today.
Live demonstration: click-by-click teaching checklist
  1. Open the working JavaScript webpage. Click the button once, reload, then ask the class to predict the next result.
  2. In VS Code open index.html and show the script src with defer. Switch to story.js and point to the futureText string, the two selectors and the click listener.
  3. Trace the connection between id=story, querySelector and textContent. Change the future sentence, save and test.
  4. Temporarily change the script filename in HTML so it does not match. Show that the page still renders but the action fails. Restore the name and retest.
  5. Show the guide's code-copy button and the exact destination file. Ask students to describe the listener before they change it.

LAB 03 / FRIDAY 30 OCTOBER 2026

The same idea in Construct

How does the tool change the way you express an interaction?

Morning · 08:15–10:15 · 120 minutes

What you can take from this lab

Leave with: Visible, saved progress on the student’s own project, using today’s technique where relevant; the starter exercise is available for anyone who needs it.

Connect to the course: Affordances and creative coding: different tools make different actions easy to build. The artistic intention can remain while the implementation changes.

Before this session: prepare and check
  • Open a fresh Construct project and the guide with the official interface screenshots. Test preview and saving a .c3p.
  • Have the class's existing licence or access arrangements ready if provided. Keep the exercise small and do not ask students to purchase a plan.
  • Prepare one Text and one Button example, plus a separate saved copy for the JavaScript version. Keep the object name Text unchanged.
  • Open the VS Code → Construct guide at steps 5–8. Do not use the web page's DOM code as a Construct action.
  • Treat the demonstration checklist as a menu: show one small example to the room and use the other items during individual support. Two breaks and own-project time are protected.
Opening words you can use
We are taking the same creative idea into another tool. In the webpage, JavaScript found an HTML element. In Construct, the event sheet can connect a condition to an action. Start by making the interaction work, then look at another way of expressing it. You do not need to become an expert in two systems today. You need to recognise where your objects are, what triggers a change, and how to test and save that change. Keep your own observation at the centre of the example. You are welcome to work on your own project throughout the working blocks. I am here to assist with whatever helps you move forward. We have two breaks, and you do not need to finish a whole project today.
Live demonstration: click-by-click teaching checklist
  1. Create a fresh project. Point out Layout, Properties, Project Bar, Event sheet and Preview using the real interface guide.
  2. Double-click the layout, insert Text, and set its text to a starting observation. Insert Button and change its displayed label.
  3. Open Event sheet 1. Add Button → On clicked, then Text → Set text. Enter the future sentence as a quoted string. Preview and click.
  4. Save a copy. In the JavaScript demonstration version, remove the Set text action and use the row's Add… → Add JavaScript command.
  5. Copy the two-line Construct action from VS Code. Explain runtime.objects.Text.getFirstInstance().text, preview and compare with the event-only result. Keep only one text-changing action on that row.

LAB 04 / FRIDAY 6 NOVEMBER 2026

Take your project to the library

Which books can help you understand, question and explain what you are making?

Morning · 08:15–10:15 · 120 minutes

What you can take from this lab

Leave with: A small research trail for the student’s own project: two candidate books, one relevant passage read carefully, a project connection and one checked Chicago author-date reference.

Connect to the course: Research is part of making. Genre, history, ethics and social justice can offer different ways to understand the same interactive work.

Before this session: prepare and check
  • Coordinate the visit and meeting point with Alinta and the library before announcing it. No librarian session or group space has been booked by this programme.
  • Check current opening hours and access for both groups. The current UiB listing gives the Arts and Humanities Library weekday opening from 08:00, but recheck before the visit.
  • Allow ten minutes for travel from the agreed starting point. If the classroom is farther away, arrange to meet at the library or revise the plan without cutting breaks or submission time. Finish at the library rather than rushing back.
  • Check candidate books in Oria before the visit. Do not promise a particular title is on the shelf. Have an ebook/open-access route ready, including Design Justice.
  • Use a discussion area for teaching and keep quiet areas quiet. Agree an accessible meeting/travel arrangement and an equivalent seated or online research route.
Opening words you can use
Today we take your project to the library. You are not here to collect impressive names or read an entire shelf. Bring one question about what you are making. Is it electronic literature? A game? Digital art? A system about memory, inequality or belonging? We will find a book that can help you think, locate one useful section and connect it to a real decision in your work. I am learning too. If neither of us knows where to look, asking a librarian is part of the method. Think of this as debugging your research question, with better chairs.
Live demonstration: click-by-click teaching checklist
  1. Open the English UiB Oria page from the library guide. Search an exact title or author first; then try one project concept with another term.
  2. Use Books and, for a physical visit, Available on the shelf where shown. Open the record and read the actual location, call number and availability. Do not guess the shelf from the subject alone.
  3. At a suitable shared table, open the title/copyright page, contents and index. Choose a promising section, read around it and decide if the argument actually fits the project.
  4. Separate an exact quotation from a paraphrase and from your own idea. Record the actual page number immediately.
  5. Build one author-date reference using the edition in hand. Match its author and year to the in-text citation; replace the page placeholder with the page you actually read.
