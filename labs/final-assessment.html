BEYOND THE SCREEN · FROM ALINTA’S LECTURE

Close your eyes. Is it still art?

Aesthetics comes from the Greek aisthetikos: “of sense perception”. Not only what looks good. Seeing is believing, but looking is only one way in. The brief even names an interactive sound piece.

SOUND AS VIBRATION

Hear

Sound travels through air, water and solids, not through a vacuum. Humans hear about 20 to 20,000 Hz.

  • River ListeningLeah Barclay with the Australian Rivers Institute, 2014–Listening to rivers, under the surface.
  • What Stirs Between?Camille Norment, 2023Visitors sit or lie down and feel the voices vibrate through wood.
  • Department of XNanna K. Hougaard, 2019An algorithm rearranges eight researchers into a conversation that never happened.

IN CONSTRUCT Add the Audio object. Right-click Sounds › Import sounds. Event: on tap › Audio › Play. A variable can set the volume.

IN YOUR FUTURE What will a future river, city or forest sound like? Make one sound the visitor can only find by listening.

TEXTURE AND PRESSURE

Touch

Museums say look, don’t touch. Digital work does not have to.

  • JabblrsBritt Ransom, 2016Silicone objects with electronics and code. Dogs play with them, with their mouths.
  • Elephant “jukebox”Fiona French, 2014–2021Elephants use their trunks on tactile controls to trigger sounds.
  • Good Night LampAlexandra Deschamps-Sonsino and collaborators, 2005–2015Switch your lamp on, and a lamp in someone else’s home lights up.

IN CONSTRUCT Touch › On hold gesture rewards patience. Drag & Drop lets them push things. Browser › Vibrate buzzes phones that support it.

IN YOUR FUTURE In your future, what can no longer be touched? Let the visitor hold, press or wait instead of just look.

Balance

INTEROCEPTION

Inside

Conditions within the body: heartbeat, breath, hunger.

Invisible

ROOM C · THE SOUND & MEDIA STUDIO

This builds on Hanna’s labs at the start of the semester: clean your sound in Audacity first, then bring sound, pictures and video into your work.

Make it sound like your future.

Bring it in. Record with the microphone button and keep the meter out of the red. Or drag a sound file into the window. Install FFmpeg so Audacity opens most formats.

Room tone. Record 10 seconds of silence first. Select it › Effect › Noise Reduction › Get Noise Profile. Select all (Ctrl/Cmd + A) › Noise Reduction › OK.

Cut. Zoom in. Select the “um” or the silence, listen, then Delete. Edit › Clip Boundaries › Split to move a part.

Level. Effect › Normalize to -1 dB. Peaks hitting red are clipping: record again a little quieter.

Blend. Effect › Fade In or Fade Out. Overlap two tracks and use Crossfade Tracks for a smooth change.

Export. File › Export › MP3. Keep the .aup3 project file so you can edit later.

THE CITATION DESK / CHICAGO AUTHOR-DATE

Chicago author-date: how to cite

Use Chicago author-date consistently for your in-text citations and References list. These examples follow Chicago 18. It has two connected parts: the short author–year citation next to the idea, and the full source in References at the end of your document. Follow any more specific course instructions.

  1. Find the source details. Author, year, title and the page or other locator you actually used.
  2. Cite beside the idea. Write (Surname Year, page). There is no comma between the name and year.
  3. Add the full source once. Choose its type below and complete the reference.
  4. Match the two. The same creator and year must lead the reader from the citation to the reference.

When you paraphrase

Explain the source’s idea in your own words, then cite it. Your own wording still needs a source.

[Your explanation of the passage] (Jørgensen 2013, [page]).

When you quote

Copy the exact words, use quotation marks and cite the page or locator. Put the citation before the sentence’s final full stop.

“[Exact words checked against the source]” (Jørgensen 2013, [page]).

Replace every [bracketed field]. These are format templates, not sentences to submit. The book and journal article below are real publications; no quotation or claim about a particular page is supplied.

Template for a formal reference. A personal creator starts Surname, First name; an organisation name is written once, in full. Identify the release or commit when it matters. Credit borrowed code, Construct templates, fonts, images and sounds in the project and your discussion. Keep the licence and requested attribution. Cite the original record.

Two authors, several authors, missing pages or the same year?
  • Two authors: (Surname and Surname Year, page).
  • Three or more authors: (FirstSurname et al. Year, page). In Chicago 18, list up to six authors in the reference; if there are more than six, list the first three followed by et al. Preserve the source’s author order.
  • Same author, same year: use 2026a and 2026b in both places, assigned by the alphabetic order of the titles.
  • No stable pages: use a chapter, named section, paragraph number when available, or recording timestamp. Do not use the browser’s printed page number as though it belonged to the source.
  • A source cited inside another source: find and read the original where possible. If you cannot, acknowledge the secondary citation and list the source you actually read.

Build your References list

  1. Order entries alphabetically by surname or responsible organisation. For the same author, order by year.
  2. Put the year immediately after the creator’s name. Invert the first personal author’s name: Surname, First name.
  3. Use italics for book, journal and stand-alone work titles. Use “quotation marks” for article, chapter and web-page titles.
  4. Use a hanging indent: the second and later lines of each entry start slightly to the right.
  5. Check creator, year, title, edition, pages and DOI against the source. Check automatically generated references too.

Chicago 18 does not require a publisher’s city for a book. Article references still need the complete page range or article number where available.
