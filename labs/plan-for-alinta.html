Every lab fits the listed 105-minute slot, with one 15-minute break: 08:15–10:00 for the morning group and 14:15–16:00 for the afternoon group. Both groups get the same lab on the same day.

Lab 4 uses the same frame but includes the walk to the library. Every part of a lab on the site shows its clock time, and students switch between morning and afternoon times with one button.

Attendance and evidence. Each lab starts with a check-in: the student writes a thought from the day's prompt, picks a colour and gets a lab pass as a PNG. They upload the pass (and a project screenshot if the task asks for it) to the MittUiB task. The site never sends anything anywhere; MittUiB is the only record.

Three steps per lab. Check in, Experiment, Save & submit. Each step earns a star on the lab map, and marking a lab complete shows a finish screen with the skills unlocked and a quote from Ada Lovelace, Charles Babbage or Alan Turing.

07

AI in the labs

AI is allowed and taught, but never required: every core activity works without generative AI.

  • Lab 2 teaches AI-assisted coding as a skill: the student writes the intention, asks for one small thing, reads every line, changes something by hand and logs what the AI did and what they did.
  • The AI coding guide follows on from VS Code and Live Preview, and links to the course's rules for declaring AI help.
  • Screenshots as a help route. When stuck in Construct, students can send a screenshot to an AI assistant, or use Google Lens if they prefer not to use AI.
  • The library lab is the "0% AI" lab. The Stacks jokes that the library has no AI and is always the best option: students find real books, read a real passage and cite the page they actually read.
  • The finish screens carry the message on purpose: "The AI wrote suggestions. The human made the decisions."
08

Accessibility and engagement

The site is built to pull beginners in without leaving anyone out.

Engagement

  • A level map with three stars per lab, a lab rank from Lab intern to Lab Director, and a "+1 star" pop when a step is done.
  • Arrival tasks tied to digital culture and the student's own screen life, with a private layer that is never saved or shown.
  • Finish screens with skills unlocked and a quote from a computing pioneer.
  • "This is a workshop, not a speedrun": students bring their own project and choose one manageable next step.

Accessibility

  • An accessibility console on every page: larger text, calmer colours, high contrast and no motion.
  • All animation stops when the device asks for reduced motion, and nothing flashes more than three times a second.
  • Keyboard access, readable contrast and tap targets of at least 44 px on phones.
  • The digital wheelchair ramp lets students feel four barriers and repair them: readable, reachable, calm and described. The screen-reader demo reads in English.
