Week 11 · Lab

What Are You Actually Trying to Make Someone Experience?

Final project feedback

First 10 minutes · project diagnosis

Everyone writes, before anything else

I AM MAKING: ________________ FOR: ________________ I WANT THE PERSON USING IT TO FEEL / NOTICE / UNDERSTAND / QUESTION: ________________ THE MOST IMPORTANT INTERACTION IS: ________________

If they cannot answer the last line, that is where feedback starts. Underneath all four: what does this make someone do that they would not have done in an ordinary conversation?

Feedback priority — work in this order

  • 1 · Concept — what is it?
  • 2 · Experience — what does the user actually do?
  • 3 · Form — why HTML / Construct / audio / whatever technology?
  • 4 · Scope — can they realistically finish?
  • 5 · Technical problem — what needs fixing?
  • 6 · Polish — only after everything else.

Your most useful intervention: scope

Too large

  • “I want to make an RPG about climate change with eight worlds.”

Same idea, stronger prototype

  • “One room where every object the player takes makes the landscape less habitable.”

Be ruthless about scope while remaining encouraging.

Questions to use, repeatedly

  • What is the strongest part of this?
  • What can you remove?
  • What does the user actually do?
  • Why does that interaction matter?
  • What happens if the user does nothing?
  • What is the first thing they see?
  • How do they know what to do?
  • What is your ending? Does it need one?
  • Why are you using this technology?
  • What is currently decoration rather than meaning?
  • What can realistically be finished?

Accessibility check, before finishing

  • Can I read it? Can I navigate it?
  • Does it work without sound?
  • Is important information communicated only through colour?
  • Does motion need an alternative?
  • Do I understand what is clickable?
  • Does it work at a reasonable browser size?

Not every beginner project needs perfect WCAG compliance. You are introducing design responsibility.

Optional Week 12 · open studio

BUILD / TEST / BREAK / FIX
  • Pair students. Person A uses Person B's work — no explanation.
  • The creator stays silent for the first 3–5 minutes.
  • Where does the tester click? What do they misunderstand? What breaks? What surprises them?
  • Switch, then write:
THE USER DID: ________ I EXPECTED: ________ THEREFORE I WILL CHANGE: ________
