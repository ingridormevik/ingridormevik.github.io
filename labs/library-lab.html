01 / START WITH YOUR WORK

Turn the project into a question.

Open this part of the field guide

Finish this sentence: “I am making ____, and I need to understand ____.” Choose one medium and one issue. Examples: interactive fiction + memory; platform game + exclusion; digital artwork + embodiment; interface + privacy; computing history + gender.

Is your work electronic literature, a game, digital art or several things at once? Treat those as questions to investigate. You do not have to force the work into one label before you start reading.

02 / SEARCH THE REAL CATALOGUE

Go from words to a location.

Open this part of the field guide
  1. Open UiB Oria. For a known book, enter its author or title. For a topic, try two meaningful terms from your question.
  2. Use the Books resource filter. For shelf browsing, choose Available on the shelf where shown.
  3. Open a useful result and read its actual location, call number and availability. Save these before walking to the shelves.
  4. Follow the location in that record. Ask for help if the copy is unavailable or you cannot locate it. An ebook or related title may be more useful than waiting for a particular book.

Official instructions: Find books on the shelves · What Oria searches. The reading suggestions below are not verified holdings or availability promises.

03 / OPEN THE BOOK WITH A PURPOSE

You do not need to start on page one.

Open this part of the field guide
  1. Title and copyright pages: record author, full title, year, publisher and edition.
  2. Contents: find a chapter that speaks to your question.
  3. Index: look for a specific concept, medium, artist or issue. If there is no index, use chapter headings or ebook search.
  4. Introduction: identify the author's question and approach.
  5. One section: read around a relevant passage. Ask what the author argues and what evidence supports it.
  6. Bibliography: save one promising lead if you need another perspective.

A useful source can disagree with your initial idea. Ask: what would I change, reconsider or explain differently after reading this?

04 / KEEP YOUR VOICE AND THE SOURCE SEPARATE

Make a research card.

Open this part of the field guide

Project question → source idea → page → my interpretation → design decision

  • Quotation: exact words in quotation marks, with the actual page.
  • Paraphrase: the idea expressed in your own words, still with attribution.
  • My connection: how the passage helps explain or challenge something in your project.

Do not turn the book's whole argument into a claim based on one sentence. Record what you have actually read, what remains uncertain and whether another source could complicate the claim.

05 / CHICAGO AUTHOR-DATE

Make the source findable.

Use the author-date system requested by the course. A short in-text citation points to the full entry in your reference list. Check the current course instructions and the official Chicago examples.

A real book reference

Hayles, N. Katherine. 2008. Electronic Literature: New Horizons for the Literary. University of Notre Dame Press.

Book details verified against the publisher's record. Cite the edition you actually use.

In your writing

For discussion of the book as a whole: (Hayles 2008).

For a particular passage: (Hayles 2008, [page you actually read]). The bracketed text is a placeholder, not a page number. Replace it before submitting. No quotation or claim from the book is being supplied here.

Check the match

  1. Author and year in the paragraph match the reference entry.
  2. The title, publication details and edition come from the book you used.
  3. A quotation is exact and has the correct locator. A paraphrase is genuinely in your own words and still credited.
  4. For a chapter in an edited collection, use the chapter author and the appropriate chapter format. Do not automatically cite only the editor.
  5. If an ebook lacks stable page numbers, follow Chicago and course guidance for a chapter or other locator. Never invent print pages.
  6. Check any automatically exported citation. Catalogue metadata is a starting point, not a guarantee.
