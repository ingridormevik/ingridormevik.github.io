Turing, illustration and audio

The quotation is the closing sentence of Alan M. Turing’s Computing Machinery and Intelligence, Mind, volume 59, issue 236 (1950), pages 433–460. Read the paper.

The cartoon scientist, pinecone SVG, mission instruments and canvas game are original lab illustrations and interactions created with AI coding assistance. The scientist is an imaginative portrayal of Turing, not an archival image. The Sims comparison uses actual published game images. The three images are thematic references, not before-and-after views of one house. The room commentary is original teaching narration, not quotations from EA or the pictured players.

Quote playback uses the visitor’s device speech synthesis. It is modern synthetic narration, not Turing’s recorded voice. Andrew Hodges’s Turing biography site notes that no recording of his voice is known. The linked 1951 computer-music restoration is documented by the British Library; it is not embedded or presented as Turing speaking. The mission instruments, robot, orbit scene, steam room, code engine and greenhouse are original SVG graphics created with AI coding assistance. Optional discoveries and XP belong only to the extra level. Lab sound effects are generated by the site’s Web Audio code.

Computing history: the time machine

Original SVG artwork and simplified interactive teaching models created with AI coding assistance. They illustrate historical ideas, not exact machine replicas. The optional restoration counter never alters course progress. Synthesised three-note sound sequences are modern demonstrations, not historical recordings. Existing gears, discrete signals and animated information paths are visual references to mechanical computing, instruction tapes, computer music and linked information. Decorative steam, floating objects and blinking eyes have been stopped.

  • Computer History Museum: Ada Lovelace’s 1843 notes
  • Computer History Museum: Babbage’s engines
  • Andrew Hodges: Turing and the 1936 universal machine
  • Smithsonian: Mark II logbook and the 1947 moth; “bug” predates this incident
  • British Library: Strachey, Manchester and the 1951 recording
  • Doug Engelbart Institute: the 1968 demonstration and team
  • CERN: the Web proposal in 1989 and first browser/server in 1990
