## Citation guidance

Chicago author-date is the requested teaching style. The supplied 105 assessment does not itself prescribe a citation system, source quota, file format, or treatment of references/captions in the word count. The guide distinguishes these from confirmed assessment requirements.

The new citation desk uses Chicago 18, including the updated three-or-more-author in-text rule, up-to-six-author reference rule, no publisher city, and optional chapter-wide page range. These are confirmed by the publisher's [18th-edition changes](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/help-tools/what-s-new.html). The official [author-date quick guide](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/tools_citationguide/citation-guide-2.html) is linked; its web-tool fetch redirected to login. University-authored library help was also consulted for source types and locators. Some university pages retain older multiple-author examples; the publisher's 18th-edition guidance takes priority.

Real examples: Kristine Jørgensen's *Gameworld Interfaces* (MIT Press, 2013), her 2012 *Games and Culture* article (DOI 10.1177/1555412012440315), and UiB's undated course page with the actual access date. Other types are visibly labelled templates. Page placeholders are never presented as quotations or evidence of a passage read. Chapter authors, editors, DOI/article pages, image captions, reuse credits and original-source discovery are distinguished.
