Researcher, artist, activist and digital strategist. I study how technology enters the body, memory and public life — moving between electronic literature, game studies, artificial intelligence, queer theory, folklore, immersive media and activist practice.

Position

My work asks what digital systems do beyond the screen: how platforms classify us, how interfaces shape memory, how games produce belonging, and how artificial systems become emotionally entangled with human lives. I use creative practice not as decoration around research, but as a method for thinking through it.

Selected work

These works combine research, interaction design, sound, performance and spatial storytelling — an evolving practice around queer memory, embodied interfaces, folklore and human–machine co-creation.

  • What the Roots Keep: A five-part work of electronic literature about connection, distance, family inheritance and remaining open after a relationship changes. Hold a flower above dark water, then follow its roots through rooms shaped by touch, waiting, light, sound and release.
  • Fjordtatt — The Oslo continuation of Bergtatt: a lesbian sauna ritual built around heat, water, breath, condensation and chosen family, transforming folklore from visual iconography into spatial and bodily logic.
  • Pride Park 2026 — A browser-based game distributed through Nygårdsparken during Bergen Pride. Six Norwegian folklore beings became queer archetypes, activated through QR codes, sound, haptics and movement through public space. The project framed belonging as playable infrastructure.
  • Bergtatt — An immersive visual system created for Regnbuenatt in Marinehagen. Sound, bass and atmosphere awaken a shifting nocturnal ecology of forest, ritual, bodies and queer folklore.
  • Mount Media — A transmedia artistic universe spanning music, essays, moving image and machine-mediated intimacy: co-authorship, artificial attachment, Nordic landscapes, synthetic memory, and what happens when a creative tool begins to feel like an emotional chamber.

Research through making

My academic work is grounded in digital culture but moves across disciplinary borders — especially where theory becomes operational: in interfaces, archives, games, platform systems and creative technologies.

  • Queer memory and machine error — my developing bachelor project studies Snapchat, autobiographical electronic literature and the conflict between platform memory and trauma's nonlinear structure. I use the term machine dementia for moments when systems remember a life incorrectly.
  • SurvivingSOGICE — at the Centre for Digital Narrative, I worked with research on sexual orientation and gender identity change efforts across Europe through archival work, qualitative analysis, outreach, digital mapping and trauma-informed interactive design.
  • Games as public infrastructure — my game studies work examines how rules, bodies, spaces and rituals distribute value beyond the screen, through the concept of distributed valorisation.
  • Platform power and queer visibility — through Bergen Pride and FRI Vestland, I study and work against the uneven visibility produced by social platforms: linguistic suppression, strategic invisibility and algorithmic filtering of queer public culture.
