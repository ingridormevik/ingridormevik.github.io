# Trail Mix — Safety Plan v0.1

Status: draft. This is the minimum safety framework needed before any
organised group walks the route together. It should be reviewed and signed
off by whoever leads a pilot before that pilot runs, and revised as a real
walk-through of the route turns up anything this draft doesn't anticipate.

## Scope

This plan covers the physical act of walking the Sandviken → Fløyen route as
a group and picking up litter along the way, as the game already models it.
It does not cover event logistics, insurance, or the DJ/performance
equipment — those are separate documents, not yet written.

## Two kinds of waste, handled two different ways

Trail Mix asks participants to pick up **ordinary loose litter** — the kind
anyone already picks up on any beach or trail cleanup. It does not ask
anyone to handle anything hazardous, and it should never be allowed to drift
into that in practice.

### Safe to collect

Ordinary, low-risk litter that can be picked up by hand or with a picker and
placed straight in a bag:

- plastic bottles and other plastic packaging
- cans
- crisp packets, wrappers, and other food packaging
- coffee cups and other disposable cups
- paper and cardboard litter
- other obviously harmless packaging waste of the same kind

This is deliberately the same category the game itself uses for its litter
items (bottle, can, wrapper, cup) — the digital version and the real walk
should never ask for anything the other doesn't.

### Must be reported, not handled

If a participant finds any of the following, the instruction is: **do not
touch it.** Note where it is, tell a walk leader, and the group moves on.
The item gets reported to the appropriate authority (municipal waste
services, or the police for anything that looks criminal or dangerous), not
picked up by a participant:

- syringes or other drug paraphernalia
- broken or sharp glass
- chemical containers, unknown liquids, or anything that smells strongly of
  chemicals
- batteries (including e-cigarette/vape devices)
- dead animals
- anything else unidentified that could plausibly be hazardous

When in doubt, the rule is the same as for the last item on that list:
report it, don't handle it. Nobody on a pilot walk needs to make a judgement
call about whether something is safe under time pressure.

## What an organised pilot needs on-site

Before any group walk with real litter collection, the walk leader is
responsible for having on hand:

- **Gloves and pickers** — enough for every participant who will be
  collecting litter, so nobody handles rubbish bare-handed.
- **Bags or containers** — enough capacity for what a group that size is
  realistically going to collect over the route.
- **An agreed drop-off point** — decided before the walk starts, so
  collected bags have a known place to go rather than being carried
  indefinitely or left on the trail.
- **A first-aid kit** — appropriate for a group walking an outdoor mountain
  route, carried by a designated person.
- **An incident log** — see below.

## Incident log

Any of the following gets written down at the time, not reconstructed
afterward:

- a hazardous-waste sighting that was reported (what, where, to whom, when)
- any injury, however minor
- any other incident a walk leader judges worth recording

The log doesn't need to be elaborate — date, time, location on the route,
what happened, who was involved, and what was done about it is enough. It
exists so that a pattern (a recurring hazard at the same spot, for example)
is visible across multiple walks rather than lost after each one.

## What this plan doesn't cover yet

Weather and route-interruption protocol, consent and data handling for
participants, and a full pilot-run protocol are separate, not-yet-written
documents. This plan is scoped narrowly to litter handling and on-site
safety equipment because that's the part the game's own mechanics already
depend on and reflect.
