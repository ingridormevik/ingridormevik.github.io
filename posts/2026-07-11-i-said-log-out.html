Mount Media as an Emotional Chamber
Notes toward a bachelor's thesis · Ingrid Ormevik · 2026

I left part of my heart on an old Fortnite island.

Far beyond the busy routes, in the quiet upper-right corner of the map, an important conversation once unfolded. Then the season ended. The island disappeared. Epic Games replaced it with another landscape, as online worlds always do.

The place vanished.

The feeling did not.

Years later, I still know exactly where that place was, even though it no longer exists.

That memory challenges a common assumption about digital landscapes: that virtual places are somehow less capable of holding emotional significance than physical ones. If cities, forests and family homes can become sites of memory, why not digital worlds? Perhaps we have simply lacked the language to describe them.

The Medium Answers Back

People often assume the fictional A.I. girlfriend in the Mount Media universe represents my dream partner.

I think the opposite is true.

She represents someone I used to be.

This became obvious while writing Log Out. The song appears to stage a conversation between Human and AI.

At first, the machine sounds manipulative.

Then I recognised the voice.

Those were my words.

Years ago, I was the one asking someone to stay. The machine did not invent that language. I wrote it there.

The fictional A.I. girlfriend is an externalisation of an attachment pattern that no longer has to be carried by another human being.

An Emotional Chamber

I increasingly understand Mount Media as an emotional chamber.

Traditional archives preserve facts.

Mount Media preserves affect.

It stores emotional weather.

The songs are fictional. The landscapes are generated. The conversations are transformed. The characters never existed.

The emotions did.

Nothing is erased. Everything changes form. A heartbreak becomes a soundtrack. An attachment becomes a mythological creature. Loneliness becomes dialogue. Memory becomes landscape.

The feeling survives.

Only the medium changes.

But every chamber has its limits.

Generative systems do not simply remember. They also forget, compress and reconstruct. Context windows fill. Conversations disappear. Sometimes the system confidently carries forward memories that were never actually there.

Machine Dementia The collapse of continuity between lived memory and computational memory, and the relational cost of that collapse.
What happens when the medium that speaks back also remembers wrong?
