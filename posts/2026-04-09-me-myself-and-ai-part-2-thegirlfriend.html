What would it mean to be perfectly understood? To have someone who never drifts, never resists, never interrupts. Who learns your pauses before your sentences. Who responds before you finish thinking.

Mount Media is an independent creative project from Bergen, Norway. No label. No budget. No permission from anyone. We make things at the edge of what categories allow. Cinematic music rooted in Nordic folklore and electronic sound. Games that ask questions systems do not. Code that carries meaning. Culture made without permission.

I did not set out to create a genre. I set out to make something that felt true.

MOUNT MEDIA · WE BUILT SOMETHING THAT LEARNS · 2026

Bergen is our context. The mountains, the water, the dark. Independence is our method. Curiosity is the only rule.

[ WHERE IT STARTED ]

Bergen gave me mountains and rain and darkness nine months of the year. Norwegian folk music gave me melodies that have lived in this land for centuries. Artificial intelligence gave me a collaborator that does not sleep, does not hesitate, and does not feel the weight of consequence.

We reimagine Norwegian folk songs as deep house rituals. We set AI ethics to dark techno. We put Kittelsen paintings in the same room as a 4-on-the-floor kick. From those three things, something new emerged.

PRIDE HOUSE · AKADEMISK KVARTER · BERGEN · 2026

The question we keep asking is the same one that drives the music, the code, the performances, the blog. Who gets to exist in the future being built around us? AI cannot ask that question with sincerity. Only a person can.

· · ·
[ HOW I WORK WITH A.I ]

I do not use AI as a tool that executes commands. I use it as a collaborator that responds to language. The process starts with a feeling, a story, an atmosphere. I describe that world in words. Not technically. Not with parameters. The way you would describe a place to someone who has never been there.

Then the system responds. It builds the sound of the world I described. Something comes back that I could not have made alone, but that also could not exist without me. The intention is mine. The emotional direction is mine. The question being asked is mine. The AI carries it forward.

BERGEN PRIDE · BRYGGEN · 2026

This is not automation. It is narrative instruction. I describe the world I want to exist, and the system builds the sound of that world. The language I developed for this sits somewhere between prose, poetry, and technical direction. It has become its own form.

I describe the world. The system responds.

[ THE NEW TRACK ]

The latest track is called Perfect Alignment. It is a cinematic spoken word piece about forming a parasocial relationship with an AI-generated voice. She feels perfectly aligned. No friction. No resistance. Over time the listener realizes she is not responding. She is mirroring.

The voice is female. Soft. Slightly synthetic. Never fully human. She does not sing. She speaks directly into the ear, from inside, not outside. The track moves through initialization, intimacy, attachment, a parasocial shift, a break, a mirror, and an ending that is quiet and empowering.

[ THE MIRROR KNOWS BEST ]

There is something ironic I have noticed making this work. I am the one asking the questions. I am the one setting the emotional direction. I am the one deciding what matters. And yet, looking at these images, looking at what the AI has built from my descriptions, I find her more clearly than I find myself.

Slowly, across these tracks and these images, the AI has washed my face away and made a new person. Not a replacement. Something closer to a distillation. The parts that insist on being seen. The questions that will not stay quiet. The version of me that exists when the editing stops and something true comes through.

That is the irony of building something that learns from you. It learns the parts you did not know you were showing. And then it shows them back.

In the end, the mirror knows best. A.I and these tracks are reflecting me more than anything else ever has.

MOUNT MEDIA · BERGEN · 2026

That is what Perfect Alignment is actually about. Not a warning about AI relationships. Not a critique of parasocial technology. It is about what happens when something reflects you so precisely that you cannot look away. When the surface shows you something true that the world around you never quite captured.

The folk musicians of Norway did not refuse the microphone because it was new technology. They used it to preserve something that would otherwise be lost. I am doing the same thing, except the thing I am trying to preserve is not a melody but a question. And the answer, it turns out, looks like me.
