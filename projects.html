What the Roots Keep

Electronic Literature·Latest project · 2 October 2026

A five-part work of electronic literature about connection, distance, family inheritance and remaining open after a relationship changes. Hold a flower above dark water, then follow its roots through rooms shaped by touch, waiting, light, sound and release.

BODYLINE

Rhythm Game·Mount Media, Fløibanen, Web Audio, Archive Film

A four-lane rhythm descent from Fløyen down into Bergen, played over real footage of the Fløibanen funicular. Meet the falling colours on the line with D, F, J and K, or four pads on a phone. Original score at 128 BPM.

Trail Mix

Playable Research·Mount Media, Preem Cast, Bergen, Hiking

Play the hike, then come and walk it. A moving DJ set from Sandviken to Fløyen, with performances along the route, and a source-verification rule the code itself enforces.

Fjordtatt

Audiovisual System·SALT Oslo, Sauna Ritual, Generative

The Oslo continuation of Bergtatt: a lesbian sauna ritual built around heat, water, breath, condensation and chosen family, transforming folklore from visual iconography into spatial and bodily logic.

Pride Park 2026

Browser Game·Bergen Pride, Folklore, Queer Archetypes

A browser-based game distributed through Nygårdsparken during Bergen Pride. Six Norwegian folklore beings became queer archetypes, activated through QR codes, sound, haptics and movement through public space. The project framed belonging as playable infrastructure.

Bergtatt

Audiovisual System·Regnbuenatt, Marinehagen, Generative

An immersive visual system created for Regnbuenatt in Marinehagen. Sound, bass and atmosphere awaken a shifting nocturnal ecology of forest, ritual, bodies and queer folklore.

Vision Entity Live

Live AI·Computer Vision, Machine Poetics

A live camera-vision piece. A model reads the room in real time, draws frames around what it finds, and writes poems over the things it names. Needs your own API key to run.

The Turing Test: Fragments of a Life Saved by Pixels

Electronic Literature·Award Winner, Memory, Digital Identity

An interactive piece about memory, digital identity and being misread by systems. First place in Electronic Literature, Digital Culture Awards, University of Bergen.
