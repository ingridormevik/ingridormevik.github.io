  1. Feb 2026

    The Turing Test: Fragments of a Life Saved by Pixels

    Interactive fiction about AI bias against Norwegian LGBTQ+ organisations, built from documented cases where language models invented crisis resources that do not exist. The interface degrades as the player moves through the failures, so the form carries the argument. First place in Electronic Literature at the Digital Culture Awards, University of Bergen.

  2. Mar 2026

    Vision Entity Live

    A live camera-vision piece. A model reads the room in real time, draws frames around what it finds, and writes poems over the things it names. A study in how machine description feels when it is pointed at you.

    Bergtatt

    A generative audiovisual system built for Regnbuenatt 2026 in Marinehagen, Bergen. Sound, bass and atmosphere awaken a shifting nocturnal ecology of forest, ritual, bodies and queer folklore. Built in two versions, one for screen and one for the venue's LED wall.

    Pride Park 2026

    A location-based folklore game distributed through Nygårdsparken during Bergen Pride. Six Norwegian folklore beings become queer archetypes, activated through QR codes, sound, haptics and movement through public space. Belonging as playable infrastructure.

    HAGEN

    A garden that listens and watches. Twenty-three named versions since 3 August, walkable in three dimensions and reactive to the room's own sound and camera, consent-gated throughout. The build history is the work: each version exists because the one before it failed in a specific way.

  9. Sep 2026

    Signal: Unknown

    Interactive electronic literature in six lexias. A masculine woman's body is drawn in particles, and the words other people place on it are tethered to the skin, so you can drag them off. Hold one button and the chest dissolves, then comes back. On keeping your breasts and your masculinity at the same time, and on the kind of discrimination that arrives as a joke. A plain text version is built in.

    Open Signal: Unknown

  10. Sep 2026

    BODYLINE

    A four-lane rhythm descent from Fløyen down into Bergen, made under the Mount Media name. The run plays over real footage of the Fløibanen funicular, muted and sped up, with the archive film credited on the page. Notes fall in four colours and you meet them on the line with D, F, J and K, or four pads on a phone. Original score at 128 BPM, with the chart and the music running off one clock, so the mountain lights up only when you are actually on the beat.
